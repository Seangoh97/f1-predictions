How it works

A pace model predicts where each driver finishes if they reach the end, from qualifying, grid slot, recent results, car and driver race pace, practice long runs, sprint results and circuit history. A second model estimates the chance of retiring from reliability, crash history, the circuit and grid slot. The rain forecast sets the chance of a wet race.

Each race is then simulated 10,000 times, with more spread and more retirements in wet runs. Win, podium and points chances mix the simulation with historical rates for each grid slot, because pole position alone is a strong predictor of the winner. The position odds come straight from the simulation, so they can differ a little from the headline figures.

Races still to run, before qualifying, use a lighter model built only on form, car pace and circuit history. The championship odds simulate every remaining race 20,000 times.

Limits

  • Lap-level pace data only goes back to 2023, so older races train without it.
  • Only about ten races since 2023 were wet, so the rain effect is a rough estimate.
  • Crashes, safety cars and failures cap how accurate any pre-race prediction can be.
  • Car setups, fuel loads and team strategy plans aren’t public.
